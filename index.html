Los Angeles County, California

Planning a teen’s hospital discharge in Los Angeles

Use this guide when a Los Angeles County hospital is preparing to discharge your teen and the care team is considering residential treatment or another next step. Bring it to the discharge meeting so the hospital, family, and receiving team leave with the same plan.

Where to start

Ask the hospital’s discharge planner to confirm the next level of care and the appropriate way to travel. Get a named admissions contact, a confirmed bed and arrival window, and a backup plan before scheduling a private ride. [1]

Go to the checklist

Get the discharge team and admissions on the same call

Ask your hospital social worker or case manager to coordinate the meeting. Bring a short list of questions and ask what your teen wants to know or have help explaining. Ask the teams to review the care and travel plan in plain language. [1]

When a California program describes itself as a Short-Term Residential Therapeutic Program, ask for the facility’s legal name and license number. Use California’s Community Care Licensing search for facilities within its scope. For another kind of program, ask which agency oversees that location and check with that agency. [2]

  • Hospital discharge plannerWho is finalizing the release time, and what travel limitations or supervision does the treating team specify? Ask the hospital whether clinical transport is required.
  • Receiving programHas the site accepted this admission and reserved a bed? Confirm the street address, intake cutoff, after-hours policy, and who is receiving your teen.
  • Family or caregiverWho will be present at release, who can sign the intake documents, and how will the teen and family be told about each step? Confirm permissions with both teams.

Prepare the day-of-transfer handoff

Ask the teams which documents should travel with the family and which should be sent directly to the receiving clinician. Keep a one-page logistics record with phone numbers, agreed times, and the name of the person responsible for each handoff.

Review the written medication and follow-up instructions with the discharge team. Ask who handles prescriptions, pending results, and questions between release and intake. Use the hospital’s approved method for sharing health records. [1]

  • At departureHave the hospital team confirm the release location and the agreed transportation plan before anyone leaves.
  • If timing slipsHave the receiving site’s direct number ready. Ask who should call the hospital if the delay changes the care or travel plan.
  • At arrivalConfirm a named staff member has accepted the handoff, the necessary records have reached the treatment team, and the family knows when it will hear from the program.

Make a Los Angeles County backup plan

If the bed or release date moves, ask the hospital for a specific interim plan, including where your teen will go, who is responsible during the gap, and who can reassess a change in condition. Confirm appointment dates and contact numbers before leaving. [1]

Los Angeles County’s 24/7 Mental Health and Substance Use Services Help Line is 800-854-7771. The county lists assistance with referrals and crisis assessment. Call or text 988 for crisis support. Call 911 for an immediate threat to life or medical emergency. [3] [4]

  • If the next step is local careAsk the discharge planner which outpatient appointment is being requested, who is booking it, and what to do if the first available date leaves a gap.
  • For youth referralsLA County publishes separate resources for children and transition-age youth. Give the county Help Line your teen’s age and ZIP code so staff can direct you to the appropriate service. [3] [5]

Before leaving the hospital

Common questions

Who arranges transport from an LA hospital to teen residential treatment?

Start with the hospital discharge planner. Ask that person to check with the treating clinician and the receiving program about the appropriate travel arrangements and who will make them.

Can I book pickup before a residential bed is available?

Ask providers for availability while you wait. Confirm the admission, release window, and travel plan with the two care teams before you commit to a pickup or buy a nonrefundable ticket.

What if the facility can’t admit my teen on the discharge date?

Call the discharge planner and admissions contact. Ask them to agree on an interim location, supervision, medication access, and a follow-up contact before the release.

What should I ask if my teen is worried about the ride?

Give your teen a chance to tell the care team what feels hard about the plan. Ask about a family ride if clinically appropriate, how the teen can contact a caregiver, and what would cause the team to pause and reassess travel.
